# Chapter 11: Business Automation

## Why It Matters
This is where OpenClaw stops being a personal assistant and becomes an execution system for real work. It connects research, planning, implementation, testing, and reporting into a business pipeline.

## Core Ideas
- The “software factory” is a gated pipeline, not a single prompt.
- Business automation works best on repeatable workflows with named outputs and clear owners.
- Research, document processing, code review, and project tracking are high-leverage fits.
- The system should compress routine coordination, not pretend human judgment is gone.

## Patterns To Keep
- Break business work into phases with explicit artifacts and summaries.
- Use operators for approvals where error cost is high.
- Treat reports, research packs, and review outputs as first-class artifacts.

## What To Adopt In OpenClaw
- Formal project folders with intake, evidence, planning, implementation, and QA artifacts.
- Business-facing pipelines should require gates before external actions.
- Keep client or stakeholder communication separate from internal system chatter.

## Risks / Limits
- Business automation often touches high-cost mistakes: customers, revenue, credibility.
- It is easy to over-automate judgment-heavy work.
- External data quality can poison downstream decisions if not validated.

## Open Questions
- Which business workflows in this repo are mature enough for automation now?
- Where do human approvals stay mandatory?
- Which artifacts should be generated automatically versus hand-curated?
